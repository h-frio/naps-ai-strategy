Innovation Board Review

A structured governance committee that turns validated opportunities into clear decisions.

The Innovation Board Review (IBR) is the monthly governance body responsible for reviewing validated AI and analytics submissions, aligning on delivery paths, and allocating resources. This page describes how the committee works — its members, cadence, meeting structure, decision flow, and feedback process.

How the IBR works
From intake to delivery decision in five steps
Every submission that clears Phase 2 triage enters the IBR pipeline. The CoE prepares the review packet, distributes it to board members, and facilitates the session. Decisions reach submitters within 5 business days.
Step 1
Packet Assembly
CoE compiles validated submissions with supporting data and preliminary priority signals
Step 2
Pre-read Distribution
Packets sent to all members 5 business days before the session
Step 3
Board Session
90-minute structured meeting with time-boxed agenda, discussion, and decisions
Step 4
Decision & Assignment
Each submission receives an outcome: Approve, Queue, Redirect, or Close
Step 5
Submitter Feedback
Structured notice with outcome, score breakdown, and improvement path within 5 days

Quorum policy: A minimum of 5 of 7 core voting members must be present for any decision to be valid. If quorum is not met, the session proceeds as a working review only — no binding decisions are recorded. Approvals are deferred to an async vote or the following session.

Decision framework
Four possible outcomes for every submission
Every submission reviewed by the board receives one of four structured outcomes. The outcome determines what happens next for the submitter, the delivery team, and the portfolio.
✅

Approved

The initiative meets the scoring threshold, fits within available capacity, and has been classified into a delivery tier. CoE assigns a delivery team or coach and sets a start date.

→ CoE assigns team · Start date confirmed · Submitter notified
🕐

Queued

The initiative is approved in principle but cannot start immediately due to capacity constraints. It retains its score and position and moves to the top of the queue for the next cycle.

→ Score retained · No resubmission needed · Next cycle priority
🔄

Redirected

The initiative has merit but was submitted on the wrong path. Common redirects: a Tier 3 submission that should be reframed as a Tier 1 POC first, or a request belonging to a different program.

→ Specific guidance provided · Resubmission encouraged
🚫

Closed

The initiative does not meet the minimum scoring threshold, has a compliance blocker, lacks a clear business case, or duplicates in-progress work. Closed items receive specific rationale and may be resubmitted.

→ Detailed rationale · Improvement path · Resubmission possible
Feedback & communication
How the board communicates its decisions
Every submitter receives structured feedback regardless of outcome. Transparency builds trust in the process and raises the quality of future submissions.
1

Decision notice

The CoE sends a structured decision notice to each submitter within 5 business days of the board session. The notice includes the outcome, assigned tier (if approved), and the delivery contact or CoE coach name.

SLA: 5 business days after session
2

Score breakdown

For all outcomes, the submitter receives a summary showing how the submission scored by dimension — revealing exactly where it performed well and where it fell short, with concrete guidance for resubmission or reframing.

Included with all decision notices
3

Improvement recommendations

Closed submissions receive specific improvement recommendations: which criteria to address, what additional information would strengthen the case, and whether resubmission makes sense in the next cycle. The goal is improvement, not rejection.

Required for all Closed outcomes
