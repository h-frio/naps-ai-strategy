Priority override
Break the glass
Certain categories of work are urgent by nature and must be elevated regardless of their calculated priority score. This mechanism is used judicially — not as a workaround, but as a recognized exception for genuine business-critical deliverables.
🚨

Priority Override

When activated, this field drops the initiative to the top of the queue immediately, superseding the calculated 1–10 priority score. The Innovation Board must confirm activation and document the justification in the decision record.

⚖️ Legal / Compliance

FTO (Freedom to Operate), LTO (License to Operate), RTO (Right to Operate), or any other regulatory/compliance deliverable that creates legal exposure if delayed. Applies at Epic or User Story level.

📅 Critical Recurring / Seasonal

Updates to data sets, fields, formulas, or other BI-owned products that support critical recurring activities — seasonal planning, regulatory reporting, or production-owned workflows that run on a fixed calendar.

🐛 Production Bug Fix

A bug in a live report, workflow, or analytics product that is actively disrupting business operations. Applies at Issue level. Scope must be confirmed as a defect in an existing production asset, not a new feature request.

⚠️ Discipline matters: When all requests are marked urgent, nothing is. Priority Override is reserved for genuine exceptions. The board should challenge override requests that don't clearly fit one of the three categories above.

Question weights at a glance

Score interpretation
Priority bands — what the score means
The calculated 1–10 score maps directly to a priority band. The board uses these bands to guide urgency and sequencing decisions. Lower numbers = higher urgency.
1 – 2.5
Higher Priority

Strong strategic and foundational alignment. High revenue or savings impact, broad scope, foundational platform, or employee development linkage. Approve fast and protect resource assignment.

2.5 – 5
Medium-High Priority

Good alignment across multiple dimensions but not maximized in all areas. Worth approving in the current cycle if capacity exists; strong candidate for the queue if resources are tight.

5 – 7.5
Medium-Low Priority

Some value signals but limited strategic or foundational alignment. Consider whether the submission can be reframed to improve scope or impact before committing resources.

7.5 – 10
Lower Priority

Weak alignment to strategic or foundational objectives. May be worth redirecting or deferring until a stronger business case can be articulated. Not a candidate for immediate resource commitment.

Delivery tier classification
Three tiers, three delivery paths
After scoring, each approved initiative is classified into a delivery tier based on complexity, dependencies, and governance requirements — not on value alone.

Board action by zone

Quick WinsApprove fast, assign to CoE-supported delivery, and protect momentum. These are ideal for near-term credibility and measurable wins.
Strategic BetsConfirm executive sponsor, sequence architecture and compliance reviews, and move only with staged governance and explicit capacity commitment.
Rethink / ReframeDo not reject immediately. Shrink scope, clarify the problem, or reclassify into a smaller POC before re-review.
Hold / DeprioritizePark the idea unless a new strategic driver emerges. These consume too much effort for too little value in the current context.

Board rules of thumb

Tier 1 defaultHigh-value opportunities with low complexity move first unless a compliance flag or override overrides.
Tier 3 thresholdAny initiative with enterprise dependency, net-new architecture, or multi-site scope triggers enhanced review and SteerCo escalation.
Queue disciplineDo not start more initiatives than capacity supports. Approved-but-queued is healthier than overloaded delivery.
